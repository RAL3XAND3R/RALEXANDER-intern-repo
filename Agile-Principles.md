# Agile Principles

## What is Agile?

Agile is a way of working that focuses on adapting to change, delivering work in smaller parts, and communicating regularly with the team. Instead of planning everything from the beginning and following that plan no matter what happens, Agile allows the team to adjust when priorities or requirements change.

Traditional project management usually follows a more fixed plan. A lot of the requirements are defined at the beginning, and changes later in the project can be more difficult to handle.

The main idea I understand from Agile is that the team should focus on delivering useful results and improving along the way, instead of trying to predict everything from the start.

## Agile Values and Principles

The Agile Manifesto is based on four main values:

* Individuals and interactions over processes and tools.
* Working software over comprehensive documentation.
* Customer collaboration over contract negotiation.
* Responding to change over following a plan.

There are also 12 principles behind these values. Some of the ideas that stand out to me are delivering useful software frequently, welcoming changes even late in development, communicating regularly with the team, and continuously looking for ways to improve.

## Scrum vs Kanban

Scrum and Kanban are both Agile approaches, but they organize work differently.

**Scrum** is more structured. Work is normally divided into fixed periods called sprints, usually lasting one or two weeks. The team plans what it will work on during the sprint and has specific roles and meetings. This can work well when a team has a relatively stable amount of work that can be planned in advance.

**Kanban** is more flexible. Instead of working in fixed sprints, tasks continuously move through different stages, such as To Do, In Progress, Review, and Done. The team can change priorities when necessary, and Kanban usually focuses on limiting how much work is being done at the same time.

In my own words, I would describe it like this: Scrum is more like planning a group of tasks for a specific period and trying to complete them during that period. Kanban is more like having a continuous flow of tasks and moving them through the workflow as they are ready.

## Why Focus Bear Uses Kanban

From what I understand about Focus Bear's workflow, Kanban makes sense because the team needs to be able to adapt to changing priorities and work on different types of tasks without being restricted by a fixed sprint.

For a team working on a real product, priorities can change depending on bugs, new requirements, feedback, or other things that need attention. Kanban makes it easier to pick up the next important task without having to wait for a new sprint.

It also gives the team a clear view of what everyone is working on and helps prevent too many tasks from being worked on at the same time.

## Reflection: Benefits and Challenges of Agile

One of the biggest benefits of Agile, in my opinion, is flexibility. Software development does not always go exactly as planned. Sometimes a requirement changes, something takes longer than expected, or a better solution is discovered while working on the task. Agile makes those situations easier to handle.

Another important benefit is communication. Since the team regularly shares progress and problems, issues can be identified earlier instead of waiting until the end of a project.

However, I think Agile can also be challenging. Having a flexible process does not mean there are no rules. If communication is poor or people start working on too many things at once, the workflow can become confusing. It can also be difficult when priorities change too often because it may be harder to maintain focus on one task.

## How Agile Can Help Me in My Role

As an intern, I think Agile can help me understand that my responsibility is not just to write code and finish a task. I also need to communicate what I am doing, ask questions when I am stuck, and make sure the team knows about problems that could affect my work.

It also encourages me to break bigger problems into smaller tasks instead of trying to solve everything at once. This is especially useful while I am still learning the codebase because I can understand one part of the system at a time and ask for feedback when I need it.

## Task: Main Differences Between Scrum and Kanban

The main difference is that Scrum organizes work around fixed sprints, while Kanban uses a continuous workflow.

Scrum is more structured and has specific roles, meetings, and planning for each sprint. Kanban is more flexible and focuses on moving tasks through the workflow as work becomes available.

Scrum can work well when the team can plan a group of tasks for a specific period. Kanban can be more useful when priorities change frequently because tasks can be reordered and picked up based on their importance.

I don't think one is necessarily better than the other. It depends on how the team works and what type of work they have.

## Agile Principle I Think Will Be Most Useful

The Agile principle I think will be most useful to me is:

**"Welcome changing requirements, even late in development."**

As a developer, especially as an intern, I think it is important to understand that a change in requirements does not automatically mean that something went wrong. Sometimes the team learns something new while developing the product, and changing the original plan can lead to a better result.
