# Roles in an Agile Team

## Key Roles in an Agile Team

An Agile team is made up of people with different responsibilities, but they need to work closely together to build and improve the product. Each role has a different focus, but the goal is shared: delivering useful and reliable software.

### Developers

Developers are responsible for building and maintaining the software. They implement new features, fix bugs, work with APIs and databases, and make changes to the codebase.

Developers also need to communicate with the rest of the team. If something is unclear or there is a problem blocking their work, it is important to raise it instead of working on it silently for too long.

### QA (Quality Assurance)

QA is responsible for checking the quality of the software. They test features, look for bugs, and make sure the application behaves as expected.

QA and developers work closely together because finding a bug is not the end of the process. The developer needs to understand the problem, fix it, and sometimes work with QA to confirm that the fix actually works.

### Product Manager (PM)

The Product Manager focuses on what the product should do and why. They help define priorities based on user needs, business goals, feedback, and other requirements.

The PM helps the development team understand what needs to be built, but they are not necessarily responsible for deciding how the code should be implemented.

### Agile Project Manager (Agile PM)

The Agile Project Manager helps the team stay organized and supports the Agile process. They can help coordinate work, identify blockers, improve communication, and make sure the team is working effectively.

The role is less about telling developers exactly what to do and more about helping the team work smoothly.

### Other Supporting Roles

Other people can also be important to an Agile team. UX designers can help improve the user experience, customer support can provide information about problems users are experiencing, and stakeholders can provide business or product feedback.

Even though these people may not work directly on the code, their input can influence what the development team works on.

## Collaboration in Kanban vs Scrum

The roles themselves do not necessarily change between Kanban and Scrum, but the way they organize their work is different.

In Kanban, the team works with a continuous flow of tasks. Developers, QA, and other team members can take tasks from the board as they become available. Priorities can also change when new bugs, requirements, or other important work appears.

In Scrum, work is normally organized into fixed Sprints. The team decides what it will work on during Sprint Planning and then works toward completing those tasks during the Sprint. There are also specific Scrum events where the team discusses progress, problems, and improvements.

From what I understand, Kanban can be a good fit for a team like Focus Bear because it allows the team to react to changing priorities without having to wait for the next Sprint.

## My Role in the Agile Team

My role is Backend Developer Intern. My main responsibility is to contribute to the backend of the application by working on features, APIs, database-related tasks, and bug fixes.

As an intern, I am also responsible for learning how the existing codebase works and following the team's development practices. This means that my role is not only about writing code. I also need to communicate clearly, ask questions when I do not understand something, and let the team know when I am blocked.

I contribute to the Agile process by taking tasks from the workflow, working on them, getting feedback, making changes when necessary, and eventually moving them toward completion.

One thing I am learning is that a task is not really finished just because the code works on my computer. It also needs to fit into the rest of the project and meet the requirements of the team.

## Collaboration With QA

One role I expect to collaborate with frequently is QA.

The work of developers and QA is closely connected. I can implement a feature, but QA needs to verify that it actually works correctly and that the changes did not introduce unexpected problems.

I can improve this teamwork by giving QA clear information about what I changed, how the feature is supposed to work, and anything they should pay particular attention to when testing it.

I can also try to reproduce and test my own changes before passing them to QA. This can reduce unnecessary back-and-forth and make it easier to identify real problems.

## Reflection

I think my role fits into the Agile team by being part of the people who turn product requirements into working software. I do not work independently from the rest of the team. My work depends on understanding what the product needs, communicating with other developers and QA, and being open to feedback.

As an intern, I also see Agile as an opportunity to learn from the team. I will probably make mistakes or approach some problems in ways that could be improved. Being part of an Agile team means being able to receive that feedback and use it to improve instead of seeing changes as criticism.

Overall, I think good collaboration is just as important as technical skills. A developer who can communicate clearly, ask for help when necessary, and work well with other roles can contribute much more effectively to the team.
