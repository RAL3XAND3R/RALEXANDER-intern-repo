# Troubleshooting Help Strategy

## When do you prefer using AI vs. searching Google?

I prefer using AI when I need to understand a problem, an error, or a programming concept. AI is useful because I can describe the problem in my own words and ask for an explanation or different possible approaches.

For example, if I encounter an error that I do not understand, I can use AI to explain what the error means and what parts of my code I should investigate.

I prefer using Google when I need specific or official information. This includes official documentation, version-specific information, exact error messages, or examples from other developers.

I also think that using both resources together can be more effective. I can use AI to understand a problem and then use Google or the official documentation to verify the information and make sure the solution is appropriate for the version or technology I am using.

## How do you decide when to ask a colleague instead?

I would ask a colleague when the problem is related to something specific to the project that I cannot understand from public documentation or general resources.

I would also ask for help when I have already tried several possible solutions and I am still blocked. Continuing to work alone for too long can waste time, so asking another developer for a different perspective can be more efficient.

Another important factor is sensitive information. If solving the problem requires sharing private project information, credentials, internal data, or other sensitive information, I should use internal documentation or ask a colleague instead of sharing that information with an external AI tool.

The urgency of the problem is also important. If the problem is blocking important work and I have already spent a reasonable amount of time investigating it, asking a colleague can be more efficient than continuing to troubleshoot alone.

## What challenges do developers face when troubleshooting alone?

One of the main challenges is spending too much time investigating the wrong part of the problem. Without another perspective, it can be difficult to recognize that the current approach is not working.

Developers can also become stuck when they do not understand an error or when there are many possible causes. Searching for information can sometimes produce many different solutions, making it difficult to determine which one is appropriate.

Another challenge is relying too much on a single source. AI can provide useful explanations and suggestions, but its answers should still be verified. Similarly, a search result may be outdated or may not apply to the version of the technology being used.

For these reasons, I think a good troubleshooting strategy is to first understand the problem, then choose the most appropriate resource based on complexity, sensitivity, and urgency. AI can help explain and explore solutions, Google and official documentation can help verify specific information, and colleagues can provide project-specific knowledge and another perspective.
